01

Brand overview

Brand name. GNEGR is built from the initials of Niño's full government name: G. Niño Emmanuel G. Redoble. Five letters, five parts of one name, condensed into something that reads like a badge rather than a byline.

Said aloud, the letters land close to "engineer" and "energy" — a coincidence worth letting the brand lean into, even though the name's real origin is simply his own initials.

Purpose. GNEGR exists to give Niño's technical work — from network configurations to shipped applications — one consistent identity to stand behind, whether it's on a résumé, a case study, or a business card handed across a table.

Mission. To apply a disciplined, systems-first approach across databases, infrastructure, and application development, building software and networks that hold up under real use — the same standard proven during his internship deployments at CEPALCO.

Vision. To grow from IT graduate into a versatile technology professional trusted to own a project end to end, from the first architecture decision to the server it finally runs on.

03

Logo rationale

Why a motorcycle

The motorcycle isn't decoration on top of the GNEGR mark — it's the argument for the whole brand. A motorcycle is one of the most honest machines there is: every part is visible, every part has a job, and if the engineering is off, it simply doesn't move. That's the standard Niño holds his own work to, whether he's tracing a concurrency bug through an asynchronous database migration or configuring a network under a CCNA framework — nothing is hidden, and nothing works by accident. The bike's forward lean captures momentum: the pace of finishing a CCNA certification, a full internship deployment, and a five-person product build inside the same stretch of years. Its independence — one rider, one machine, full control — mirrors the freelance and mentoring work he's carried alongside full-time study since 2021. And in the original mark, the wordmark doesn't sit on the bike as a label; it's built into the chassis itself, the letters forming the body the wheels carry. That single detail is the whole case study in miniature: his name isn't attached to the work, it's the structure underneath it.

Typography

One typeface locked to the mark, one built to complement it, one for reading, one for code.

Setup note
Blanka is a free display font by Troisième Type (troisiemetype.gumroad.com/l/blanka) — the same file used to set the wordmark in the logo itself. Save your licensed copy next to this document as fonts/Blanka-Regular.woff2 (or .otf) so the specimen below renders correctly; until then it falls back to Sora automatically.
BLANKA — wordmark only
GNEGR

The exact typeface built into the logo — minimalist, geometric, uppercase-only, designed for huge sizes. It has no lowercase and a very small character set, so its job stops at the mark. Never reset it as a headline, a UI label, or body copy, and never rebuild the wordmark in a substitute font.

Sora — display / headings
Full-stack development. Network engineering.

Chosen specifically to sit next to Blanka: the same squared-off, monoline geometry and technical confidence, but with a full weight range and complete character set Blanka doesn't have. Carries every H1–H3 in the system.

Why this pairing
Blanka is a specialist — a minimalist display face built for exactly one job at huge sizes, which is exactly why it works inside the mark and exactly why it can't carry a paragraph. Sora sits beside it without competing: related geometric DNA, but built as a full working typeface with the weights and glyphs a real layout needs. Inter and JetBrains Mono continue underneath, handling reading and code the way they always have.

Color palette & visual system

A disciplined warm-motorsport palette built for clarity, high energy, and uncompromising consistency across light and dark viewports.

06

Design principles

Hierarchy
Lead with type weight and size before color. On any single view, only one element should carry an accent color.
Spacing & grid
An 8px base unit (8 / 16 / 24 / 32 / 48 / 64 / 96). Body copy in a column no wider than about 720px. Margins scale from 24px on mobile to 96px on wide desktop.
Contrast & accessibility
Body text meets at least 4.5:1 contrast; large text and icons meet at least 3:1. Color is never the only carrier of meaning. Focus states stay visible; motion respects reduced-motion preferences.
Consistency
Keep typeface roles fixed — Blanka in the mark, Sora in headings, Inter in body, mono for technical labels. Don't introduce a fifth typeface, and don't reassign a face to a job it wasn't chosen for.
Responsive use
The mark should scale predictably — full lockup at large sizes, down to a simplified icon at small sizes — with full construction rules maintained in the dedicated logo system reference. Layouts drop from multi-column to single-column below 720px, and navigation collapses to a simple scrollable list.
07

Brand voice

Professional, confident, precise, authentic, quietly bold, forward-looking.

Do

  • Use plain verbs and concrete specifics — name the stack, the certification, the deployment
  • Let finished work carry the confidence, not adjectives
  • Write the way he'd explain a project to another engineer

Don't

  • Reach for hype language — no "rockstar," no "ninja," no "passionate"
  • Overstate scope beyond what actually shipped
  • Bury a real detail under jargon

The one rule
Blanka does exactly one job — the mark, and nothing else. Everywhere else, Sora leads, Inter reads, and mono documents the stack. That division is what keeps four typefaces from ever feeling like four typefaces.
